## What We're Building

### The Core Concept
**A travel intelligence service powered by real local knowledge that exists nowhere else online.**

While the interface is a simple WhatsApp conversation, we're building a proprietary knowledge graph of operational intelligence sourced from real people who know these places. The AI is just the delivery mechanism. The knowledge quality is the differentiation. The knowledge graph is the moat.

**The product works across your entire trip journey**:
- **Pre-trip**: Adaptable planning based on who you are
- **On-trip**: Your plan adapts in real-time to what's actually happening
- **Post-trip**: Validation loop improves the knowledge graph

**What makes this different isn't the personality or interface—it's the quality and depth of knowledge that comes from real people who actually know these places.**

### The Market Signal
**Travelers already bypass platforms to find trusted humans.** They text friends, post in Facebook groups, DM people who've visited. They're not looking for another algorithm—they're searching for someone who actually knows the city. Paul doesn't create demand. Paul replaces a broken, fragmented workaround with a proper solution.

### Four Problems We Solve Simultaneously

1. **Trust Collapse**: Travel information has never been more abundant or less trustworthy. TripAdvisor is gamed. Google ratings are fake. Instagram is sponsored content. Travelers have learned platforms can't be trusted.

2. **Expertise Gap**: Knowing WHERE to go vs. knowing HOW to go. Every traveler has this story: amazing recommendation, couldn't find the entrance, didn't know it was cash only, ordered wrong, sat in the wrong section. Recommendation was right. Execution failed.

3. **Decision Fatigue**: 4-day trip in KL = 12 meals + 50-80 micro-decisions. By day 2, most travelers default to whatever's closest or what the hotel concierge suggests (who gets commissions).

4. **Belonging Gap**: The isolation of being an outsider. Having Paul say "tell uncle you want kurang manis, he'll appreciate it" transforms the interaction. You're not a clueless tourist. You're someone who knows.

### The Identity Insight
**People aren't buying better restaurants. They're buying access to an identity they desperately want**: the person who has a "guy" in every city. The person who travels like an insider. This is incredibly aspirational and takes years to earn legitimately. Paul gives instant access.

Critically, they can perform this identity for others: "Skip Jalan Alor, go to this stall in Kampung Baru, cash only, order the dry version." Their social capital goes up. **That's what they're really paying for.**

### Knowledge as Instruction Set, Not Content
Every piece of local knowledge becomes **functional**, not informational:
- "Cash only" → Paul locates nearest ATM before routing you there
- "Closed Mondays" → Paul never recommends it on Monday
- "Google pin wrong" → Paul sends corrected coordinates
- "Better before 11am" → Paul messages you at 10:30am

This is what makes Paul an **agent**, not a chatbot.

### The Contributor Model
**People who love traveling already love sharing what they know.** Right now they share it in DMs, Instagram stories, Google reviews, Reddit threads—scattered and unsearchable.

We're structuring knowledge they already want to share via low-friction WhatsApp voice notes. Contributors aren't creating content (Thatch's mistake). They're answering questions they've already answered 100 times: "Where should I eat in Lisbon?" "What's the best neighborhood in Bangkok?"

**Incentives**: Recognition (your recommendation helped 23 travelers), reciprocity (free roaming when you travel to other cities), and protection (favorite spots won't be overrun).

### The Waze Playbook
Google acquired Waze for $1.1B in 2013. They didn't buy the app. They bought it because **every day they didn't own that data, it was being used against them.** Waze had 50M users contributing real-time road intelligence Google couldn't replicate by throwing engineers at it.

**Our thesis**: Build a contributor-driven knowledge graph so valuable that acquiring it becomes cheaper than competing with it.

By Year 3: 15 cities × 300 spots × 8 operational data points = **36,000 verified intelligence points** that exist nowhere else. Replacement cost for an acquirer: $3-5M + 18-24 months they can't compress.

### Depth Over Width
**Extraordinary in a few cities beats mediocre in many.**

We will NOT try to cover every city. We will be exceptional in a few. 300 verified spots with 8 operational data points each (2,400 pieces of intelligence) in KL is the moat—not 30 cities with tourist-trap recommendations scraped from Google.

### The Adaptive Planning Model

**What makes this fundamentally different from Mindtrip/Layla**:

**Their approach** (Static Itinerary):
- Generate fixed day-by-day plan pre-trip
- Plan breaks on contact with reality (weather, delays, exhaustion)
- No memory between trips
- Optimized for booking commissions

**Our approach** (Living Plan):
- **Pre-trip**: Create adaptable framework based on who you are
  - Learn preferences through conversation (not questionnaire)
  - Generate tiered dossier (not rigid schedule)
  - Personalized but flexible

- **On-trip**: Plan adapts in real-time
  - Weather changes → swap outdoor for indoor options
  - You're exhausted → suggest something nearby and chill
  - You loved hawker food → find more similar spots
  - Behavioral learning → gets smarter about your taste

- **Post-trip**: Close the validation loop
  - "How was that spot?" → captures freshness
  - "What did you discover?" → adds new knowledge
  - Travelers become contributors naturally

**The insight**: This is what a real human travel expert would do. Framework upfront, adapt based on reality.

### The Tiered Dossier Format

Instead of a rigid itinerary, we deliver a **personalized intelligence dossier**:

**Structure**:
```
BANGKOK - Your Personal Guide

━━━ TIER 1: CAN'T MISS ━━━
(3-5 spots you absolutely shouldn't skip)
• Each with full operational intelligence
• Why it matters, when to go, what to order, pro tips

━━━ TIER 2: SHOULD DO ━━━
(10-15 spots organized by type + neighborhood)
• Breakfast | Lunch/Dinner | Activities | Evening
• Filtered for your preferences and trip length

━━━ TIER 3: GOOD TO KNOW ━━━
(20+ backup spots and hidden gems)
• "If you're in the area" options
• Weather backups
• Time-of-day alternatives

━━━ SUGGESTED FLOW ━━━
Day 1: Chinatown immersion
Day 2: Riverside & temples
Day 3: Modern Bangkok
Day 4: Revisit favorites

(But we'll adapt this on-trip based on reality)
```

**Why this works**:
- Substantial enough to feel valuable
- Flexible enough to adapt
- Shows knowledge depth (the differentiation)
- Works in WhatsApp (text + links)
- Prioritization is clear

### Mercedes vs Toyota

Even though we compete with Mindtrip/Layla in the same space (pre-trip + on-trip planning), **we're fundamentally different quality**:

| Dimension | Them (Toyota) | Us (Mercedes) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Knowledge source | Scraped Google/Yelp | Human-sourced from locals |
| Quality | "Good enough" generic | Deep operational intelligence |

**The market reality**: "Good enough" doesn't exist yet. Mindtrip still hallucinates (suggests closed hotels). Layla is booking-focused with rigid itineraries. GuideGeek has no memory. **The window for quality is still open.**

**The window won't stay open forever**: 12-18 month head start before someone else (or them) figures out human-sourced intelligence. The race is to build the knowledge graph moat fast enough.

## How to Execute

### MVP Approach: Pre-Trip Heavy, On-Trip Light

**The Strategy**: Build and use it ourselves first (dogfooding), then test with external travelers.

**Phase 1: Build & Dogfood (Weeks 1-6)**

**Pre-trip focus (80% of effort)**:
1. **Conversational profile learning**
   - Natural conversation infers preferences
   - Not rigid questionnaire
   - Learns: travel style, budget, pace, dietary needs, interests

2. **Tiered dossier generation**
   - Pull from knowledge graph
   - Personalize based on profile
   - Generate must-do / should-do / nice-to-have tiers
   - 20-40 curated spots with full operational intel

3. **Knowledge graph seeding**
   - Founders contribute 50-100 spots via voice notes
   - 10-20 friends add their knowledge
   - Target: 100-200 spots with depth for one city
   - Test contribution flow while building

**On-trip focus (20% of effort)**:
1. **Basic context awareness**
   - Weather API integration
   - "It's raining → here are covered/indoor options"

2. **Simple replanning**
   - User: "We're tired, need something close"
   - Agent: Filter by location + vibe, suggest accordingly

3. **Feedback loop**
   - "How was it?" → captures quality + freshness
   - Builds post-trip validation data

**Phase 2: Test with External Travelers (Weeks 7-10)**
- Give 20-30 friends pre-trip dossiers
- Light on-trip support
- Collect feedback post-trip
- Validate: Is quality noticeably better? Would they pay?

### Group Travel Approach

**Key insight**: Most group trips have one organizer who coordinates preferences.

**Our model**:
- **Leader acts as intermediary** between group and agent
- Leader: Gathers group preferences → communicates to agent
- Agent: Helps leader make sense of conflicting preferences → generates plan
- Leader: Shares plan with group

**Not this**: Agent in group chat (too noisy, privacy concerns, coordination complexity)

**Why this works**:
- Matches real-world behavior (there's always "the planner" in friend groups)
- Cleaner interaction model
- Leader can pre-filter before sharing with group
- Phase 2 feature (start with solo/couples first)

### What We Build / Don't Build (MVP Boundaries)

**We Build**:
- ✅ Pre-trip profile learning + dossier generation
- ✅ On-trip weather awareness + basic replanning
- ✅ Post-trip feedback loop (validation)
- ✅ One city with depth (100-200 spots)
- ✅ Conversational knowledge contribution (voice notes)
- ✅ WhatsApp exclusively (no app, no website)

**We Don't Build Yet**:
- ❌ Complex behavioral learning (tracking patterns over multiple interactions)
- ❌ Proactive notifications ("leave in 15 min")

- ❌ Multiple cities (prove it in one city first)
- ❌ Group coordination features (solo/couples first)
- ❌ Post-trip memory/recap (just feedback loop)
- ❌ Rigid itinerary generator (we're NOT competing on that)

### Case Study: Thatch—The Cautionary Tale

Thatch ($5.2M funding, 36 employees) tried to build a marketplace for travel guides. Sold to Mindtrip in March 2025 after fundamental failure.

**What went wrong**: Selected for the wrong contributors—travel influencers who visited many cities but knew none deeply. People who actually knew cities (locals, regulars) had no audience and no reason to spend hours building guides. Then AI made packaged guides worthless overnight.

#### The 7 Lessons

| Thatch's Mistake | Paul's Solution |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Built for creators, not knowledge | Extract from people who don't realize they have value (90-second voice note) |
| Static products that decay | Living knowledge with freshness timestamps, confidence scores, decay curves |
| Asked for too much effort | Contribution feels like texting a friend, not building a storefront |
| Incentivized with money (most earned <$5/hr) | Recognition, reciprocity (free roaming), spot protection |
| No verification or trust system | Every data point has provenance, recency weighting, feedback integration |
| Knowledge had no action layer | "Cash only" triggers ATM lookup; operational intelligence |
| Let knowledge get absorbed anonymously | Contributors remain credited, can withdraw knowledge anytime |
